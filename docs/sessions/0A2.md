## 2. Deviations from the design, with reasons

1. **The test world's temple** sits at (0, 0) over the 20-level legacy stack (DECISIONS: "in the test world the legacy
   fixture stays at (0, 0)"), instead of TECH_DESIGN's "fixture temple with a 3-level dungeon". Its footprint is the
   parent's 48×48 frame at x 16..63, the plan at (2, 2) in it as the parent had it, and its `dungeonRect` is the frame's
   corner (16, 0) instead of `clamp(entrance.x − 24, 0, 32)` (which would be 29): legacy level 1 lies 1:1 under the
   frame, so falls through the shaft land where they always did. The plan says so (`dungeonAt`).
2. **No sinkholes over the legacy stack** (`sinkholes: false` on the temple plan): a map-only player could otherwise
   fall into the dungeon before finding the cube. The shaft is the only hole. The ruin has sinkholes.
3. **The temple got a front door** (`P`, out of the mosaic room to the east, where the road arrives): the parent's
   temple had no way out. `templeFixture(seed)` stamps the plan the parent's way (48×48×10, rock roof, door walled up)
   and matches the golden temple block for block; on the surface the roof is a slab, the sanctum open to the sky.
4. **`returnPoint` dropped**: coming up from legacy level 1 lands beside the entrance stairs at the bottom of the shaft.
   The golden temple comparison leaves out that one field (`TEMPLE_DROPPED`, with a note); the bot now checks the
   arrival is beside `zone.entrance` (it used to check only that you were back in 0,0,0).
5. **The mosaic ring** round the altar is written into the plan rows: the parent computed it with `Math.atan2`, which
   generation may not use. `check-determinism` has no exemptions left.
6. **Cliffs raise by 5**, not 4: with up to 3 blocks of relief on either side, a 4-block rise can leave a 2- or 3-block
   step at its foot that the map doesn't draw as a wall. 5 keeps every cliff step at 4 or more.
7. **The flatten falloff** is a clamp, not a blend: beyond the skirt the height may differ from the datum by at most 1
   block per tile, so the natural ground (at most 3 blocks) comes back within the 4 tiles. A linear blend over 4 tiles
   can make a step over 1 where the relief is steep.
8. **Rivers near edges are world functions**: the channel runs straight in 8 tiles from its port on both sides of the
   edge, and its banks are part of `heightAt`, so both zones agree to the block; the zone lays only the bends between
   the approaches (5 tiles or more from the edges), with one ford per river, or a lake for a single port. A river port
   uses `hashInt(seed, edgeId, "river")` (the road keeps the design's formula), moved apart from a road port on the
   same edge.
9. **Navigability counts ground, not scenery**: a column whose standing spot is the top of a tree or of a plan's wall
   or roof is left out (nobody walks there, and the map draws it as a wall). Mesa tops count.
10. **Site dungeons ending in a guardian**: on a dungeon's last level a guardian's hall has no stairs behind its gate
    (`gen/dungeon.js`); before, every attempt failed with "stairs down on the last floor".
11. **Landmark dungeons list each floor's level** (bible §15.3 gives them; the Unlit Choir starts below its surface
    level), instead of a base level and a step.
12. **A road or a landmark opens a span its terrain would shut**: travel "road" (only along the road, 3 hours) and open
    edges (the Molar pass, the Drowned Lantern). A river does not (the Inkrun ends in the sea).
13. **An edge is open only when both spans have open edges**, so the answer is the same from either side.
14. **The real world has no regions yet**: a span's region is its terrain, and no village, ruin or lair is placed
    there. Lairs and ruins have no bottom yet (WS10); history's site requests are not placed yet.
15. **The world fixture is 40×20 for the test world** (TECH_DESIGN), 42×21 for the real one (DECISIONS); both pass the
    size check. At span (19, 15) the bible's binding tier grid says 4 where its formula gives 3; the grid wins.
16. **`game.run`** holds the run (TECH_DESIGN §2.17 puts `run` beside `state`, saved with the slot by
    `serializeRun`); `game.state.world` records the world's id and version. `game.world` is the definition new runs
    use (the test world).
17. **The apron is filled by `zones.js` `fillApron(game, zone)`, not inside `Zone.refresh()`**: a zone doesn't know
    its run, and its bands come from other zones in `game.zones`. `enterZone` fills every band on each entry (so a
    neighbour you changed while you were in it is copied fresh), `makeZone` rebuilds the one band facing a neighbour
    that arrives later, and `dropDerived` drops the apron with the other derived arrays. `APRON_BUILDERS.surface`
    takes a fourth argument, the neighbour zone or null.
18. **Open corners**: a corner band exists when both of its sides are open and the diagonal span can be walked into
    from one of them. A corner without a band is solid to `solid()` and air to `castRay` (the ray goes on to the
    apron's end).
19. **Apron hits are lit as open sky** (ambient + full sky light, the torch as usual) instead of drawing dark: the
    apron has no light arrays, and `sampleLight` outside the grid gives 0, which drew it near black — a seam you'd
    see pop when crossing. That is the only shading done (sun, haze, the horizon plane: WS3). Rays that miss below
    the horizon still draw black.
20. **Arrival accepts the mirrored tile even when the anchor can't reach it**: you can always step back the way you
    came. A mountain span's pocket by its edge (test world 27,2, seed 42) otherwise shifted you 10 tiles away. The
    other band tiles still need the anchor. In 3D, arrival keeps your exact point (`opts.exact`) when the mirrored
    tile qualifies, so the camera doesn't move; if the apron was built from the world functions and the body
    collides in the real zone, you stand up on the tile's centre.
21. **The four straight neighbours of the zone you are in keep their derived arrays** (`keepDerived`), so the swap
    never waits for a `refresh()`; that is up to 2.4 MB more. `zone.reach` caches what's reachable from the anchor
    (by `navVersion`) for edge arrivals.
22. **Messages**: a solid edge logs the neighbour terrain's `edgeMessage` (or "You can't go on into <terrain> from
    here."), and the world's border "Nothing lies past this edge of the world.", once per visit and side. The zone
    name is logged on crossing until 0D1's toast. Thrown things still stop at the zone's border (they never leave it,
    like creatures).
