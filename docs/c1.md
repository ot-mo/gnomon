## Integration requests

Each is code or a frozen file, so it's the integrator's; the data for it is in place on `ws/c1`.

1. **Villages by quota** (`world/sites.js`, WS10's). Bible §4.10 asks for exactly 16 villages, by region (Flats 1,
   Waste 1, Steps 2, Downs 2, Pleatwood 2, Deep Pleat 1, Tessellate 2, Shade 1, Stacks 2, Glass shore 1, Teeth 1).
   `placeSites` rolls a chance per eligible span, so the real world gets 9 to 25 a seed (15.2 on average over 40
   seeds; check-story's 20 seeds: 10 to 23). Wanted: before the chances, place each region's `REGIONS[r].villages`
   on free eligible spans of that region drawn from the region's own stream (a region without `villages`, the test
   world's three, keeps P0's draws, so golden and the test world stay as they are); then `sites.village` can go to 0
   on the real world (C1b, or the merge). The same for ruins (14) and lairs (12) once §4.10's block rule ("exactly one
   guaranteed site per block") is built; until then their chances give 14.5 ruins and 10.9 lairs a seed.
2. **The Teeth's crag village** (`world/sites.js`). §4.10 and §9.4 put a village in the Teeth ("caves and eyries up a
   cliff"); Teeth spans are walk-in only (travel "no"), which `placeSites` never makes eligible. Wanted: a region's
   quota may use its walk-in spans (edges open). Until then `teeth.sites.village` is 0 and its quota of 1 unplaced.
3. **A region's biome** (`world/worldmodel.js` compileWorld: `biome: R.biome || T.biome || terrain`). The Glass Shore's
   34 spans are Tessellate or Stacks by terrain, so they dress and populate as those; with the line above and
   `glassShore.biome = "sea"` (C1b can add it, or the merge) they become the sea's shore (glass crust, glass shards,
   glass crabs and salamanders, brine ponds: gen/terrain.js liquidOf reads the biome's liquid, which is what
   `glassShore.liquid` says). It changes those spans' surface generation only.
4. **The world-map line** (`overworld/renderWorld.js` spanLines). Each region has a `line` (bible §2.1 rule 8: one
   sentence for a world-map tile; §15.9's 600 words for tile lines, rumours and deaths): shown dim under the region's
   name in the span panel.
5. **The sky** (optional, `clock.js`/`render3d.js`, WS3's). `REGIONS[r].sky` names what the bible gives each place:
   "glare" for the Salt Waste and the Glass shore (bible §4.9: from 11:00 to 13:00 the 3D view drops to 6 tiles and
   colours bleach; the map is unaffected), "canopy" (the Pleat's green dimness), "ashen" (the Shade, already done by
   its terrain's `dark`), "crystal", "haze", "clear". Nothing reads it yet.
6. **Versions** (the integrator's call, as the plan says). The regions are new fixed map data for the real world, and
   they move its runs' sites (villages now) and its spans' music, fog and encounter rates; Hollowmere's plan and the
   wildlife counts change its surface generation. What I'd do: bump `WORLD.version` in `worldmap.js` to 2 at the merge,
   so `restoreRun` refuses a real-world run saved before C1a (all of them `?world=real` previews, whose sites have no
   villages); and leave `VERSION.world` and `VERSION.gen` (config.js) as they are, since the test world's map and
   generation don't change (its terrains take `DEFAULT_BIOME`, which C1a left alone), so test-world saves keep
   loading. If the last wiring merge bumps `VERSION.world` anyway, the per-world bump is still the one that matters.
7. **The cube on the real world** (`main.js`, at the last wiring merge, as HANDOFF already plans). Hollowmere's temple
   is the parent's, cube on its altar, Maren at her place and you waking in your room; `?world=real`'s start step
   (`story.cube = true`) now gives a second cube's worth of story: drop it when the real world becomes the default.
8. **Villages by region, not terrain** (`story/villages.js` line 61, WS7's; for C2). It reads
   `VILLAGES.byTerrain[tile.terrain] || …[tile.region]`, so a Glass-shore village (terrain tessellate or stacks) gets
   the Tessellate's page-town or the Stacks' storeys. With C2's tables by region (its ids are C1a's), read the region
   first.

9. **A site span's surface costs a dungeon level** (`gen/surface.js` landingFloor, WS2's; the timed gate). A span with a
   dungeon under it generates that dungeon's level 1 to place its sinkholes, about as much again as the surface itself
   (on `p3i-props` the test world's ruins: 55-68 ms against 22-29 for plain spans). The real world had no per-run
   sites before C1a; now perf's seed-4242 sample holds one, a lair at (22, 3), and check-perf's surface generation reads
   p95 68.8 ms against `p3i-props`' 45.5 (×1.51, budget 60). C1a's data doesn't make a zone dearer (the region rows give
   the same level time as the legacy table). What I'd do: keep that level 1 for the descent (the zone of `wx,wy,1` is
   the same level: zones.js could take it instead of making it again), or place the sinkholes from the level's plan
   without stamping it; either is code. The integrator's call whether C1a tags with this open.
